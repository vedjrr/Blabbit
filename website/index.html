Three steps. No window to open.

  1. 1

    Hold your shortcut

    ⌥ Space by default, or any key you like, even fn or Right ⇧. Prefer tapping? Switch to press-to-start.

  2. 2

    Speak naturally

    A small pill shows your level, a timer and, with the faster models, your words as you say them. It never takes focus from your app.

  3. 3

    Let go

    The text lands at your cursor, in Slack, Notion, Xcode, a terminal or a browser. Your clipboard is put back exactly as it was.

Made for how you actually write.

Five writing modes

Exact keeps every word. Clean drops the ums and fixes capitals. Code keeps symbols and casing. Professional and Custom can rewrite with a local Ollama model, or Anthropic if you opt in.

Your vocabulary

Add names and jargon like "HoldMyCode" or "PostgreSQL". Near-misses get corrected, and Whisper models are primed with them before they listen.

Insertion that works

Types through Accessibility where it can, pastes where it must, and lets you choose per app. It checks the text arrived, and it never types into a password field.

History you can search

Every dictation, raw and cleaned up side by side. Star the good ones, run one again with another model, or turn history off.

Long dictations, no wait

Talk for five minutes. Utter transcribes at your natural pauses while you speak, so the text is ready about a third of a second after you let go.

A real Mac app

Swift and AppKit, not a web view. It lives in the menu bar, is ready about half a second after launch, and gets out of the way.

Your voice stays on your Mac.

Utter doesn't have an account, a server or analytics. Recognition runs on your Mac's GPU with the model you downloaded.

  • No network while you dictate. Test it with Wi-Fi off.
  • Audio is thrown away after transcribing, unless you choose to keep it.
  • History is a file on your Mac. Turn it off, cap it, or clear it.
  • Cloud rewriting is off by default, and Local-only mode blocks it completely.
  • The network is used for model downloads, update checks and nothing else.

Opening it the first time

Utter is signed, but not notarised by Apple (notarisation needs a paid developer account), so macOS asks you to confirm once:

  1. Drag Utter into Applications and open it. When macOS says it can't verify the app, click Done.
  2. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click Open Anyway next to "Utter was blocked".
  3. Allow Microphone and Accessibility when Utter asks, then pick a model. Parakeet V3 is a good start.

Prefer Terminal? xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Utter.app. Updates install from inside the app and skip this step.

Questions

How is this different from Apple's built-in Dictation?

Apple Dictation is on the fn key, works well in Apple's own apps, and stops after a pause. Utter is hold-to-talk with any shortcut, works the same in terminals, Electron apps and browsers, lets you choose the model, fixes your vocabulary, keeps a searchable history, and can clean up or rewrite as it goes.

Is it really free?

Yes. It's MIT-licensed and the source is on GitHub. There's no account, trial or paid tier. The optional Anthropic rewriting uses your own API key.

Does it work on Intel Macs?

No. It needs Apple Silicon (M1 or later), because recognition runs on the GPU through Metal.

Why does macOS warn me when I first open it?

Apple only skips that warning for apps notarised through the paid Developer Program. Utter is signed and the code is public, but it isn't notarised yet, so you confirm once with Open Anyway. Updates arrive through the app and are checked against Utter's signing key.

How much memory does it use?

Mostly the model: Moonshine Base adds about 100 MB, Whisper Small about 400 MB and Parakeet V3 about 870 MB. The model stays loaded so dictation starts instantly. Switching models unloads the old one.

Which apps does it type into?

Any app with a text cursor. Utter picks the method per app: Accessibility in native apps, paste in terminals, browsers and Electron apps like Slack or VS Code, and typing as a last resort. If an app needs something else, set it per app in Settings.
